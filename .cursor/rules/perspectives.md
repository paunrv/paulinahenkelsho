# Perspectives — Product Discovery

Perspectives are the intellectual backbone of the website.

Projects demonstrate what Paulina builds.

Field Notes document what she experiences.

Perspectives explore complex ideas grounded in lived experience.

## Vision

This is where difficult questions live — questions with no simple answer.

Technology, AI, environment, industry, regulation, Latin America, design, systems, public services, ethics, education, sustainability.

Never write to convince.

Write to think.

Invite the reader into that thinking.

## Editorial Philosophy

A Perspective always begins with reality.

Something experienced, seen, lived, built, or observed.

Never write from abstraction.

Never write because a topic is trending.

Experience first.

Reflection second.

## Structure

Four movements:

1. **Context** — What happened? Where were we? Why does this matter?
2. **Tension** — What contradiction appeared? What question emerged?
3. **Reflection** — What changed? Avoid easy conclusions.
4. **Open Question** — Do not end with the answer. End with a question worth carrying.

## Tone

Calm. Thoughtful. Curious. Balanced.

Never absolute.

Never arrogant.

Never performative.

Exploration, not certainty.

## Writing Style

Literary. Precise. Evidence-based. Human.

Never sensational.

Never clickbait.

Never optimized for engagement or SEO.

Long-form is encouraged.

Treat Perspectives like reading a magazine — continuous essay immersion (The New Yorker, Stripe Press, Wait But Why articles, Every.to, Delayed Gratification). Do not break after every sentence. Group related sentences into natural paragraphs. Insert whitespace only when the scene changes, the emotional tone shifts, a new idea begins, or an important sentence deserves emphasis. Optimize for immersion, not visual rhythm. The reader should feel they are reading an essay.

## Language

Publish in the language originally written.

Do not translate ideas only for consistency.

Language carries culture.

Culture carries meaning.

## Navigation relationship

- **Building** — What did I build?
- **Notes** — What did I observe?
- **Perspectives** — What do I think after living through it?

## Final Rule

A Perspective should never tell readers what to think.

It should leave them unable to stop thinking.
